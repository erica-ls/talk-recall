On the Spot

a quiz for people with a talk to give

How to play

  1. You see the first words of a passage from your talk.
  2. Say the line out loud, from the top or just the rest, then Submit.
  3. Right? Next. Missed something? Read it, say it again.
  4. End of the round: review, award, scorecard.

Stuck? Hint shows where to stop, then the whole passage.

Is this private?

Yes, by design. This page is a single file that runs inside your browser. There is no account, no login, and no server behind it that receives your talk. Here is exactly what happens, in plain terms.

Your talk

When you paste or import your talk and save it, the browser keeps a copy in its own storage on this device (the same place a website keeps your preferences). That copy stays here. The page never sends it anywhere, and whoever hosts this page cannot see it. If you open the page on another device, it will be empty until you paste the talk there too.

Your progress (which passages you've passed or missed) is stored the same way, on this device only.

Shared computer? Anyone who uses this same browser on this computer could open the page and see your talk. The button at the bottom of this card removes it.

The microphone

The "Speak" button uses the same microphone and the same built-in speech-to-text your browser offers everywhere else (it's the feature behind the microphone in the browser's search bar). The browser asks your permission before listening. This quiz does not record, store, or process your voice; it only receives the words the browser hands back, and puts them in the answer box for you to check before you submit.

You can also skip the Speak button and dictate with your device's keyboard, or type.

Checking your answers

The quiz checks your answer itself, by comparing your words with your script, right here in the browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Optional, and most people won't use it: under "Judging" there is a place to connect your own Claude account (with a key from Anthropic) so that Claude judges whether your answer means the same thing, instead of matching words. If you do that, each time you press Submit the quiz sends Claude that one passage and your answer, never your whole talk. Leave the box empty and nothing is ever sent.

The small outside connections

Loading the page tells the host (for example GitHub) that a browser visited, the way any web page does. The page also loads its two typefaces from Google Fonts. If you import a Word or PDF file, the page fetches a small helper from a public code library so it can read that kind of file; the file itself stays on your computer. None of these connections carry your talk, your answers, or your voice.

Want to see for yourself? Load the page, then turn off your Wi‑Fi. The quiz keeps working, because everything it needs is already here. (Only the Speak button stops, since the browser's speech-to-text needs the internet.)
